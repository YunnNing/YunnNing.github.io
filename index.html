Team of 5. I built the core schema and the transactional logic: scheduling, booking, cancellation, reporting.

Scheduling detects conflicts with different tolerances — two hours for a bus, four for a driver. One is mechanical turnaround, the other human rest. Every operation writes to an audit table including failures, because a trail that only records successes can't tell you who tried something they shouldn't have.

the piece I'd walk through on a whiteboard

One booking can hold tickets across several schedules, so cancellation status is only knowable once the statement finishes. A compound trigger handles it: the row-level phase collects affected booking IDs, the statement-level phase recalculates each once. A simple row trigger would query a mutating table or write the wrong status.
