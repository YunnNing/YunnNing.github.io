Team of 5. I built the core operational schema and the transactional logic: scheduling, booking, cancellation, and reporting.

A bus can't run two overlapping schedules. A driver can't be double-booked or assigned on an expired licence. Cancelling a schedule has to cascade through every ticket attached to it.

The scheduling procedure uses two hours for a bus, four for a driver — one is mechanical turnaround, the other human rest. Every operation writes to an audit table, including failures.

the piece I'd walk through on a whiteboard

One booking can hold tickets across several schedules, so status can't be computed row by row. A compound trigger: the row phase collects booking IDs, the statement phase recalculates each once.

The bug above, in full. The trigger even creates a sequence and never uses it. Correct on the happy path, wrong the second run — the defect I look for first now.

Team of 4. I owned the ingestion and streaming layer: two of three scrapers, the cleaning stage, and the Kafka producer and consumer.

Articles publish to a Kafka topic, land in HDFS as Parquet, are word-counted through MapReduce, enriched with definitions and POS tags, and load into Neo4j as a graph. Cron drives incremental runs.

the design decision I'd expect to be asked about

Kafka looks wrong for a batch pipeline until you notice scheduling is why you need it. Articles published between runs sit durably in the topic until the next job consumes them.

Why the retrieval failed. Malay is morphologically rich, so exact-match queries miss when the stored form carries different affixes. The fix is stemming at query time, not more scraping.
